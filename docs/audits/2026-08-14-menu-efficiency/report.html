01The core imbalance

The kitchen maintains 57 live prep recipes to serve ~14 composed dishes plus sides and kids items — roughly four preps per dish. A brunch menu at this scale runs comfortably on 35–40. The overage isn't spread evenly: it's concentrated in single-dish projects, an 11-recipe tail that no menu dish touches at all, and an in-house bake program that competes with suppliers you already use.

The table below maps every composed dish to the prep recipes it actually touches — from the real recipe links, followed through nested preps (so the brisket picks up the coffee rub inside the smoke, and the tallow inside the aioli). "Unique" preps exist for that one dish only — their entire batch-make labor is carried by a single menu line. Mix % and margin are from the 42-day sales pull.

KEEP The platform assets — protect these

The benedict platform — Buttermilk Biscuits + Hollandaise + Hash — is the best engineering on the menu, and the sales prove it: Hash feeds seven dishes, and the four dishes riding the platform (Coastal, Ranchero, Dubliner, Elaho) are the top four sellers — 43.8% of everything sold. Same for Smashed Avocado, Crispy Onions and B&B Pickles (shared across handhelds). One correction from the recipe links: the Pork, Fennel & Chilli Sausage is not a platform asset — Elaho and Side Sausage plate a purchased Legends sausage, and the house recipe reaches no dish at all (it's in the dead list below). The strategy is unchanged: route more sales through fewer, shared preps.

02Tier 1 — The garnish tax CUT

Fine-dining garnish projects riding on brunch dishes. Each is a real batch-make with its own station time, and each is invisible to why the customer orders the dish. This tier needs no sales data — the dish survives untouched in the customer's mind.

French Toast garnish program CONFIRMED — ARCHIVE

Matcha Tuile and White Chocolate Snow are still live prep recipes, but the recipe links show no dish uses them (legacy Tonka Snow is already archived). Either they're already off the plate — then archive them — or the line still makes them off-list, which is exactly the leak to close. The flavor core the sales justify: compote, crème pâtissière, and the Coffee-Orange Maple (which the links confirm is live on both French Toasts).

The French Toast program earned its keep in the data: FT + ½ + kids = 8.8% of mix, and its brioche slice + custard also anchor the Wolf Cub (5.0%). Simplify the plate, never the dish.

Salmon-skin chicharron & mustard "caviar" ALREADY GONE

Neither exists as a prep recipe — the menu descriptions oversold the plates. The Coastal's recipe is salmon + curing mix + the biscuit platform; the Dubliner's is fully shared components (and fresh apple, not a pickle). No action beyond updating menu copy if it still promises them. The Caper & Dill Crème Fraîche the Coastal description mentions reaches no dish either — it's in the dead list.

Blood Pudding, rice version BUY OR CUT — NOW QUANTIFIED

A 240-minute house-charcuterie project that sold 25 units in 42 days — $113 of margin, the worst labor-to-revenue line on the menu. Buy a quality black pudding (your meat suppliers carry them) or drop the side entirely.

03Tier 2 — Duplicated bases CONSOLIDATE

Preps that exist twice under different names, or families that can share one mother base. Pure list-shrink: no dish changes at all.

Two pulled porks RESOLVED — ALREADY ONE CHAIN

The recipe links settled this: Adobo Pulled Pork is a sub-prep inside Smoked Pulled Pork — the Ranchero and Side Pulled Pork use the smoked batch, which folds the adobo in. Neither is legacy and there's nothing to merge; at most, collapse the two recipe cards into one if the adobo step doesn't need its own par.

Two house hot sauces → one CONFIRMED — MERGE

Hot Sauce "Verde Brasa" reaches no dish; Hot Sauce (Mexican Style) feeds the Ranchero, the Smash Burger, and the $1 ramekin (113 sold). Keep Mexican Style, archive Verde Brasa.

The aioli family → one mother base MERGE 4 → 1+

Burger Aioli, Beef Tallow Aioli, Sun-Dried Tomato Aioli (Pesto Rosso), Mayo — four emulsions. Make one house aioli base in volume; fold in flavors (tomato/pesto, spice) as 2-minute line derivatives. Beef Tallow Aioli is the worst offender: it requires a second prep (rendering Beef Tallow) as its input — a two-step chain for one sandwich. The brisket won't miss it if the burger aioli steps in.

Butter projects TRIM

Smoked Miso-Honey Butter isn't even linked to the Biscuit & Jam recipe anymore — it reaches no dish (dead list). Clarified Butter feeds the hollandaise platform across three dishes: keep.

04Tier 3 — Make vs. buy BUY

You already buy croissants, pain au chocolat, brioche (Snow Cap), the sweet-potato-jalapeño brisket bun (Sysco), burger buns, tortillas, naan, and pita. The line between "bake in-house" and "buy" is already drawn pragmatically — these three items are on the wrong side of it.

House English Muffins BUY

A sourdough whole-wheat english muffin baked in-house for one sandwich (the Cheekye). Snow Cap already delivers your GF english muffins. Either buy the regular ones from them too, or move the Cheekye onto the buttermilk biscuit you already bake daily — arguably a better sandwich and one more dish on the biscuit platform.

Hand-cut fries BUY OR CONSOLIDATE

Cut–blanch–double-fry is a daily station on its own, serving 3–4 items. Two options: a premium frozen kennebec-style fry (guests genuinely can't tell at brunch volume), or go to one potato program — you already make Hash for six dishes; brisket and burger can plate with hash or slaw. The double potato program is a luxury for a kitchen that's drowning.

Sourdough loaf + starter IDENTITY CALL

Daily starter feeding and baking supports the Elaho (9.6% of mix), Forager (3.1%), Side Toast, and — via the starter — the Cheekye's english muffins (5.8%). That's real reach, so this is not a dead program; it's the biggest passive-time item on the list (24 h each for starter and loaf). If the bread is part of Fergie's identity, keep it. If it's merely good bread, Snow Cap delivers daily. Decide on identity, not habit — but decide.

05Tier 4 — The menu calls, now made with real sales

The standing rule: a composed dish under ~4% of food-item mix that carries 4+ unique preps gets cut or rebuilt from shared components. With 42 days of sales and the real recipe links, the verdict on all three candidates is the same — no dish gets cut. Two escaped because their true unique-prep count was lower than the menu descriptions suggested; the brisket escaped on sales. The labor savings in this review come from the dead-prep tail and Tiers 1–3, not from shrinking the menu.

Texas Brisket — 6.2% mix, $17,102 margin KEEP

822 sold in 42 days (~20/day) at a $20.81 margin — fifth-highest margin total on the menu. The 6 unique preps and the second smoker program are confirmed real (the links even surfaced a seventh dependency: Pickled Jalapeños lives inside the tallow aioli and slaw). But at 1.5× the 4% threshold, the program pays for itself. The "burger absorbs it at better margin" theory also died in the data: burger margin is $20.80 — identical to the penny. Cutting the brisket risks $17k per 6 weeks to save one smoke cycle and ~5 h of batch work.

Forager Tartine — 3.1% mix, $6,209 margin KEEP (rule doesn't trigger)

Below the 4% line, but the cut rule needs 4+ unique preps and the real count is 3 — Mushroom Ragout, Charred Pesto, Vegan Yolk. The Vegan Parm and pickled crosnes the description promised aren't in the recipe (parm is in the dead list; crosnes don't exist anywhere). The dish is already the "rebuilt from shared parts" version the first draft proposed — it rides the shared sourdough. It's also the only vegan main and the lowest margin of the composed dishes ($14.89): keep, and treat it as first out if a new dish needs the slot.

Caesar Salad — 1.6% mix, $4,393 margin KEEP — BUY THE CROUTONS

The "4 unique preps, nothing shared" premise was wrong: bacon jam is shared with the Dubliner, capers are a raw item, kelp isn't a prep, so the real unique load is 2 — Croutons (120 min) and Caesar Dressing (45 min, currently missing from the recipe rows: relink it so the plate cost stops lying by ~a dollar). At ~5/day with a $20.15 margin it earns its slot at that load. Buy the croutons and the dish drops to one unique prep; revisit only if mix falls further.

Dead preps — resolved from the recipe links ARCHIVE 8 · VERIFY 2 · RELINK 1

The suspected-dead list changed a lot once checked against real links. Alive and staying: Dukkah (Shakshuka), Vinegar Reduction (hollandaise platform), House Salad Dressing (Side Greens), Pickled Jalapeños (brisket chain). Already archived: Roasted Pepper Relish, Tonka Snow.

  • Archive now — zero reach, confirmed: Scones · Pork, Fennel & Chilli Sausage (120 min — Elaho plates purchased Legends) · Caper & Dill Crème Fraîche (1,440 min passive) · Hot Sauce "Verde Brasa" · Matcha Tuile · White Chocolate Snow · Smoked Miso-Honey Butter · Vanilla Crème Pâtissière ×5 (a scaled duplicate of the live crème pât).
  • Verify with the chef: Vegan Parm and Pickle Apples — menu copy implies them (Forager, Dubliner) but no recipe links them. If they're still plated, relink; if not, archive.
  • Relink: Caesar Dressing — obviously on the plate, absent from the recipe rows.

Each archived PREP recipe is also a linked inventory item — retiring these 8 shortens every stock count and the daily run sheet.
