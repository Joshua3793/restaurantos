01The core imbalance

The kitchen maintains 58 house prep recipes to serve 14 composed dishes plus sides and kids items — roughly four preps per dish. A brunch menu at this scale runs comfortably on 35–40. The overage isn't spread evenly: it's concentrated in single-dish garnish projects, duplicated sauce bases, and an in-house bake program that competes with suppliers you already use.

KEEP The platform assets — protect these

The benedict platform — Buttermilk Biscuits + Hollandaise + Hash — is the best engineering on the menu: three preps carrying three top-priced dishes plus sides. Same for the Pork, Fennel & Chilli Sausage (Shakshuka + Elaho), Smashed Avocado, Crispy Onions and B&B Pickles (shared across handhelds). Nothing below touches these. The strategy is not "prep less of everything" — it's route more sales through fewer, shared preps.

02Tier 1 — The garnish tax CUT

Fine-dining garnish projects riding on brunch dishes. Each is a real batch-make with its own station time, and each is invisible to why the customer orders the dish. This tier needs no sales data — the dish survives untouched in the customer's mind.

French Toast garnish program CUT 3 PREPS

Matcha Tuile, White Chocolate Snow, and legacy Tonka Snow — plus an infused Coffee-Orange Maple — all decorate one $23 sweet. Keep the compote and crème pâtissière (the flavor core); plate with plain maple or keep the infused syrup only if it's batched monthly.

Evidence this dish sells and should stay: you built a ½ portion, a kids' side, and it anchors the Wolf Cub. Simplify the plate, never the dish. A tuile program alone is 30–45 min of pastry hands per batch.

Salmon-skin chicharron (Coastal Benedict) CUT

Dehydrate-and-fry project for a crunch garnish. Crispy capers already exist for the Caesar — reuse them here, or crispy onions. The lox, crème fraîche, and hollandaise are why people order it.

Mustard "caviar" (Dubliner Benny) CUT

Pearled mustard seeds simmered and rinsed in batches — swap for a smear of grainy mustard folded into the bacon jam or straight on the biscuit. Zero perceived loss on a benny.

Blood Pudding, rice version BUY OR CUT

A full house-charcuterie project supporting a $3 add-on and a $5 side. Buy a quality black pudding (your meat suppliers carry them) or drop the add-on entirely.

This is the single worst labor-to-revenue line on the menu: hours of production capped at $5 of ticket price.

03Tier 2 — Duplicated bases CONSOLIDATE

Preps that exist twice under different names, or families that can share one mother base. Pure list-shrink: no dish changes at all.

Two pulled porks → one MERGE

Adobo Pulled Pork and Smoked Pulled Pork both live on the prep list; the menu describes one preparation ("18-hour smoked pork in adobo"). If one is legacy, retire it; if both genuinely run, fold the adobo seasoning into the smoked batch and finish to order.

Two house hot sauces → one MERGE

Hot Sauce "Verde Brasa" and Hot Sauce (Mexican Style) — both fermented/cooked projects, both retailing at $1 a ramekin. Keep the one the Ranchero needs.

The aioli family → one mother base MERGE 4 → 1+

Burger Aioli, Beef Tallow Aioli, Sun-Dried Tomato Aioli (Pesto Rosso), Mayo — four emulsions. Make one house aioli base in volume; fold in flavors (tomato/pesto, spice) as 2-minute line derivatives. Beef Tallow Aioli is the worst offender: it requires a second prep (rendering Beef Tallow) as its input — a two-step chain for one sandwich. The brisket won't miss it if the burger aioli steps in.

Butter projects TRIM

Smoked Miso-Honey Butter exists only for the $7 Biscuit & Jam; Clarified Butter feeds hollandaise (keep); Garlic/compound variants as needed. If the miso butter isn't a signature guests talk about, plain whipped butter and better jam does the job.

04Tier 3 — Make vs. buy BUY

You already buy croissants, pain au chocolat, brioche (Snow Cap), the sweet-potato-jalapeño brisket bun (Sysco), burger buns, tortillas, naan, and pita. The line between "bake in-house" and "buy" is already drawn pragmatically — these three items are on the wrong side of it.

House English Muffins BUY

A sourdough whole-wheat english muffin baked in-house for one sandwich (the Cheekye). Snow Cap already delivers your GF english muffins. Either buy the regular ones from them too, or move the Cheekye onto the buttermilk biscuit you already bake daily — arguably a better sandwich and one more dish on the biscuit platform.

Hand-cut fries BUY OR CONSOLIDATE

Cut–blanch–double-fry is a daily station on its own, serving 3–4 items. Two options: a premium frozen kennebec-style fry (guests genuinely can't tell at brunch volume), or go to one potato program — you already make Hash for six dishes; brisket and burger can plate with hash or slaw. The double potato program is a luxury for a kitchen that's drowning.

Sourdough loaf + starter VERIFY

Daily starter feeding and baking supports the Forager, Elaho toast, and Side Toast. If the bread is part of Fergie's identity, keep it. If it's merely good bread, Snow Cap delivers daily. Decide on identity, not habit — but decide.

05Tier 4 — Menu cuts, pending your sales mix VERIFY

These change what the customer sees, so they wait on a 4–6 week Toast product mix. The rule of thumb: a composed dish under ~4% of food-item mix that carries 4+ unique preps gets cut or rebuilt from shared components.

Forager Tartine — $19, 5 unique preps CHECK MIX

The lowest-priced composed dish carrying the highest unique-prep density: Mushroom Ragout, nettle pesto, Vegan Yolk, Vegan Parm, pickled crosnes. If it's under ~4% of mix, replace with a vegan dish built from shared parts (mushroom ragout on sourdough with charred pesto — keep two preps, drop three). You keep a vegan option; you lose a program.

Caesar Salad — 4 unique preps CHECK MIX

Dressing, croutons, crispy capers, kelp — nothing shared with any other dish. A caesar on a brunch menu is usually a courtesy item. If mix is low, cut it and the whole program goes; if it sells, at minimum buy the croutons.

Texas Brisket vs. Smash Burger — two smoker programs CHECK MIX

You run two long-smoke proteins (brisket + 18-hr pork) and two beef handhelds. The Ranchero's pork smoke is safe — it's a signature. But if the brisket's mix doesn't justify a 12-hour smoke plus its 6 unique preps, the Smash Burger absorbs its sales at a better margin and the burger's supporting cast is already shared. This is the single biggest potential labor cut on the menu.

Suspected dead preps AUDIT

These are on the prep list but appear in no summer menu description: Scones, Dukkah, Roasted Pepper Relish, Vinegar Reduction, Tonka Snow, House Salad Dressing (side greens only), Pickle Jalapeños. Some may be catering-only — tag them so they're prepped for catering orders, not on the daily cafe cadence. Any with no usage at all: archive.

07How to verify with your own data

  1. Pull a fresh product mixToast → 4–6 full weeks (the June 1–10 window used at menu setup is too short and pre-summer). Or in-app: Sales page per-recipe quantities, since menu recipe names match Toast names exactly.
  2. Score each composed dishMix % × contribution margin (price − plate cost, which the recipe book computes live). Sort. The bottom third that also appears shaded in the Section 01 table is your cut list.
  3. Check station load in Smart PrepThe planner already tracks active minutes per prep item — sum by station for a week to confirm where the hours actually go before and after cuts.
  4. Retire in the systemDeactivate cut MENU recipes; archive orphaned PREP recipes (each is a linked inventory item — retiring them also shortens counts). Tag catering-only preps out of the daily cadence.

08Suggested rollout

  1. This week — no menu reprint neededTier 1 garnish cuts + Tier 2 merges + dead-prep audit. The menu text doesn't change; ~10 preps come off the list.
  2. Next 2 weeks — supplier movesSample bought english muffins / black pudding / fries against your own. Switch what passes. Pull the product mix meanwhile.
  3. Next menu print — the verified cutsAct on Forager / Caesar / Brisket per the mix data. Rebuild, don't just remove: every replacement dish must be composed from preps that already exist.
  4. Standing rule going forwardNo new dish adds more than one new prep, and every new prep must serve two dishes within a menu cycle. This is the rule that keeps the list at 38 instead of drifting back to 58.
